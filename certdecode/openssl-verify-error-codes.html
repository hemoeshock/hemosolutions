OpenSSL verify error codes, decoded

Almost every TLS client on the planet — curl, Git, Node.js, Python's ssl module, Java, Go's crypto/x509 — ultimately asks OpenSSL the same question. OpenSSL answers with a number. Learn the handful of numbers that matter and the rest of this site becomes a lookup table.

How to read the error

error:20 at 0 depth lookup: unable to get local issuer certificate
PartMeaning
20The X509_V_ERR_* code. This is the part that identifies the problem; the text after the colon is a human-readable rendering of the same code.
at 0 depthWhere in the chain the failure happened. Depth 0 is the leaf — the certificate for the host you asked for. Depth 1 is the intermediate, and so on.
lookupThe operation that failed: chain building. Other phases report differently (check certificate, verify error).

A failure at depth 0 usually means the leaf itself is the problem. A failure at depth 1 or 2 with certificate has expired points at an expired intermediate — the leaf may be perfectly current while its issuer has lapsed.

OpenSSL 3.x also prints a TLS-layer error string that looks scarier than it is:

error:1416F086:SSL routines:tls_process_server_certificate:certificate verify failed
error:0A000086:SSL routines::certificate verify failed

That is the wrapper, not the reason. The hex payload (0A000086, 1416F086) is an internal reason code, and the useful detail is the verify error that follows it in the output — or the one you get from running openssl verify yourself.

Get the reason on demand

openssl s_client -connect example.com:443 -servername example.com \
  -verify_return_error -verify 5 </dev/null

-verify_return_error makes the handshake fail on a verification error instead of continuing, and -verify 5 sets the maximum chain depth. To check a chain you already have on disk:

openssl verify -CAfile root.pem -untrusted chain.pem leaf.pem
openssl verify -verify_hostname example.com -CAfile root.pem leaf.pem

openssl verify prints the same error NN at D depth line, plus the file name, so you can iterate on a chain locally without touching the network.

Chain and trust errors

CodeNameWhat happenedFix
18 DEPTH_ZERO_SELF_SIGNED_CERT The leaf is self-signed. Nobody vouches for it. Issue it from a real CA, or trust the CA that issued it. Details.
19 SELF_SIGNED_CERT_IN_CHAIN The chain contains a self-signed CA that you do not trust. Add that CA to the trust store, or serve a different chain. Details.
20 UNABLE_TO_GET_ISSUER_CERT_LOCALLY No certificate found for the issuer named in the chain — a missing intermediate, or a CA missing from your store. Server sends the full chain, or client installs the CA. Details.
21 UNABLE_TO_VERIFY_LEAF_SIGNATURE Only one certificate was supplied and it is not self-signed, so there is nothing to verify the signature against. Supply the intermediate (usually the server's fault). Node surfaces this as UNABLE_TO_VERIFY_LEAF_SIGNATURE.
2 UNABLE_TO_GET_ISSUER_CERT The issuer could not be fetched (a CRL or auxiliary fetch failed). Usually transient; retry, then check the chain file.
37 INVALID_NON_CA A certificate was used as a CA but its basic constraints say it is not one. Use a proper CA certificate, not a leaf, to sign other certificates.
27 CERT_UNTRUSTED The certificate is explicitly untrusted in the trust store. Remove the block entry, or get a valid certificate.
23 CERT_REVOKED The certificate has been revoked. Replace it. Revocation is not a misconfiguration.
22 CERT_CHAIN_TOO_LONG The chain exceeds the configured maximum depth. Shorten the chain; the server is sending certificates the client does not need.

Date and validity errors

CodeNameWhat happenedFix
10 CERT_HAS_EXPIRED The certificate's notAfter is in the past. Renew it. If it renews and still fails, the client is caching the old certificate — check your intermediate cache.
9 CERT_NOT_YET_VALID The certificate's notBefore is in the future. Usually clock skew on the client. Compare date -u with the server's clock, and fix NTP.

Two date problems are worth separating. An expired leaf is a renewal failure and always shows at depth 0. An expired intermediate shows at depth 1 or 2 and is much easier to miss: the leaf dates look fine, the site looks fine in most browsers, and only clients that rebuild the chain from scratch fail.

If a certificate claims to be valid but your client says it is not yet valid, check the clock before you touch the certificate:

date -u
openssl x509 -in cert.pem -noout -dates

Name mismatch errors

CodeNameWhat happened
62 HOSTNAME_MISMATCH The hostname you connected to is not in the certificate's SAN list.
63 EMAIL_MISMATCH Same check, for an email address in a certificate.
64 IP_ADDRESS_MISMATCH You connected to an IP address and the certificate does not contain a matching iPAddress SAN.

Modern clients check the Subject Alternative Name list and ignore the Common Name. Three practical consequences:

  • A certificate with the right CN but no SAN is rejected. Adding a SAN to the certificate fixes it.
  • Connecting to an IP address needs an iPAddress SAN; a DNS: entry for the same IP does not count.
  • A wildcard covers exactly one label: *.example.com matches api.example.com but not a.b.example.com and not example.com.

These errors are about identity, not trust. Installing another CA will not help — only a certificate with the right name will.

Policy and constraint errors

CodeNameWhat happened
25PATH_LENGTH_EXCEEDEDA CA in the chain has issued more sub-certificates than its basic constraints allow.
34UNHANDLED_CRITICAL_EXTENSIONThe certificate has a critical extension the client does not understand.
47 / 48PERMITTED_VIOLATION / EXCLUDED_VIOLATIONName constraints on an intermediate excluded the name you asked for.
32KEYUSAGE_NO_CERTSIGNA certificate was used to sign another certificate but is not allowed to.

These are rarer than the trust errors, and they usually indicate a genuine mistake in how a private CA issued something rather than a client misconfiguration.

Chrome in particular collapses most trust failures into ERR_CERT_AUTHORITY_INVALID, so the browser alone will not tell you which of codes 18, 19, 20 or 21 you are looking at. Reproduce it with openssl s_client or curl -v to get the precise reason.

Quick decision table

If the error saysGo to
error 18 at 0 depthSelf-signed leaf — issue it properly or trust the CA.
error 19 at 0 depthSelf-signed CA in the chain — add that CA to your store.
error 20 at 0 depthNo local issuer — incomplete chain or missing CA.
error 21 at 0 depthNo local issuer — the server sent only the leaf.
error 10 at 0 depthExpired leaf — fix renewal.
error 10 at 1 depthExpired intermediate — replace the intermediate, keep the leaf.
